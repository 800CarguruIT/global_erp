# Leads Management

## End-to-End Service Request & Vehicle Lifecycle Guide

## Who Should Read This?

- Service Advisors
- Workshop Managers
- Operations Managers
- Branch Managers
- Company Administrators

## 1. What Is Leads Management?

Leads Management is the structured process that converts a customer service request into a completed and delivered vehicle service.

It begins when a customer requests assistance and ends when the vehicle is handed back to the customer (Car Out / Delivered).

This module connects:

- Customer profile
- Vehicle profile
- Inspection
- Cost estimate
- Job execution
- Parts procurement
- Inventory movement
- Billing and reporting

It ensures every service request is traceable, controlled, and properly documented.

## 2. Where the Process Starts

The process always begins from the Customer Dashboard.

Customer Dashboard -> Service Request -> Create Lead

This ensures:

- The correct customer is linked
- The correct vehicle is linked
- The service request is recorded properly
- All future actions remain connected

Never create operational work outside the lead flow.

## 3. Creating a Lead (Standard Intake Flow)

### Step-by-Step Overview

- Open Customers
- Select the correct customer
- Open Customer Dashboard
- Click Service Request
- Select or register the vehicle
- Choose lead type
- Enter complaint or request details
- Select branch and service context
- Save

Once saved:

- A lead reference number is generated
- The case enters operational tracking
- It becomes visible to assigned teams

## 4. Lead Types and Their Operational Purpose

Lead type determines how the workflow will proceed.

### A. Workshop Lead

Used when the vehicle requires inspection or repair at a workshop.

Typical flow:
Inspection -> Estimate -> Job Card -> Execution -> Delivery

Best used for:

- Mechanical repairs
- Electrical diagnostics
- Preventive maintenance
- Complex service jobs

### B. RSA (Roadside Assistance) Lead

Used for emergency roadside requests.

Typical flow:
On-site dispatch -> Immediate support -> Resolve or escalate

If unresolved:
Escalate to Recovery or Workshop flow.

### C. Recovery Lead

Used when vehicle transport is required.

Typical flow:
Dispatch recovery unit -> Transport vehicle -> Continue workshop process

Used when:

- Vehicle cannot move
- Accident towing required
- Breakdown requires relocation

## 5. Full Lead Lifecycle (Operational Flow)

This is the complete lifecycle from intake to delivery.

### Step 1: Lead Creation

- Linked to customer and car
- Complaint is recorded
- Branch is selected
- Lead reference generated

### Step 2: Validation & Assignment

- Service team reviews request
- Ensures details are complete
- Assigns to appropriate branch/team

This prevents misrouting and confusion.

### Step 3: Inspection

- Technical evaluation performed
- Findings documented
- Required parts and tasks identified

Inspection must occur before final costing.

### Step 4: Estimate Preparation

- Based on inspection findings
- Includes parts, labor, and additional charges
- Submitted for required approval

Do not proceed with major work before approval (as per policy).

### Step 5: Job Card Creation

- Approved estimate converts into a Job Card
- Work scope becomes structured and trackable
- Tasks are assigned to technicians

This is where execution becomes controlled.

### Step 6: Procurement (If Needed)

If required parts are not available:

- Procurement request initiated
- Supplier process executed
- Purchase order generated
- Goods receipt updates inventory

Work may pause under “Waiting for Parts” status.

### Step 7: Work Execution

- Technicians execute tasks
- Status updated during progress
- Labor and parts consumption recorded

Operational transparency is critical here.

### Step 8: Quality Check & Final Review

- Final inspection performed
- Open issues verified
- Job marked ready for release

This ensures customer satisfaction and quality assurance.

### Step 9: Car Out / Delivered

- Vehicle handed back to customer
- Lead and job marked completed
- Final reporting updated

This formally closes the operational lifecycle.

## 7. Operational Status Flow (Typical Pattern)

Statuses may vary by company, but common structure includes:

- New
- Assigned
- Under Inspection
- Estimate Pending Approval
- Job In Progress
- Waiting for Parts
- Ready for Delivery
- Delivered / Car Out
- Closed

Consistent status usage ensures:

- Clear workload visibility
- Accurate reporting
- Operational accountability
- Reduced confusion

## 8. Critical Control Points

To maintain operational discipline:

- Always link correct customer and vehicle
- Capture clear complaint description
- Perform inspection before estimate
- Do not start major work without approval
- Track parts requirements early
- Keep status updated in real time
- Complete quality check before delivery

Skipping steps causes reporting gaps and operational risk.

## 9. Business Impact of Proper Lead Management

Well-managed leads result in:

- Faster service turnaround
- Clear technician accountability
- Reduced billing errors
- Better inventory planning
- Higher customer satisfaction
- Accurate profitability tracking per job

Leads are the operational engine of the service business.

## Executive Summary

Leads Management begins at:

Customer Dashboard -> Service Request

It flows through:

Inspection -> Estimate -> Job Card -> Procurement (if required) -> Execution -> Quality Check -> Car Out / Delivered

It is the structured operational backbone that transforms customer requests into controlled, traceable, and financially recorded service completion within the Global ERP system.
