# Procurement Management

## End-to-End Procurement Flow Guide

## Who Should Read This?

- Procurement Officers
- Workshop Managers
- Inventory Controllers
- Finance and Approval Teams
- Company Administrators

## 1. What Is Procurement Management?

Procurement Management controls how required parts and services are sourced from vendors with cost, quality, and timeline control.

It starts when a valid requirement is raised and ends when goods are received, matched, and ready for operational use.

It connects:

- Estimate and job requirements
- Parts quotes and vendor comparison
- Purchase order creation
- Goods receiving
- Inventory updates
- Financial matching and reporting

## 2. Where Procurement Starts

Procurement should start from a valid operational requirement, typically from estimate or job card part needs.

Common path:

Estimate or Job Requirement -> Inquiry -> Quote Comparison -> Approved Vendor -> Purchase Order -> Receive Goods

Never create purchase orders without a traceable business requirement.

## 3. Procurement Lifecycle (Start to End)

### Step 1: Raise Requirement

- Collect required item details and quantity
- Validate urgency and branch requirement
- Confirm whether stock is already available

### Step 2: Create Inquiry

- Send inquiry to one or more approved vendors
- Include specifications, quantity, and required delivery date
- Track inquiry reference

### Step 3: Receive and Compare Quotes

- Capture vendor quotes with price and lead time
- Compare commercial and service factors
- Record selection rationale

### Step 4: Approval and Vendor Selection

- Apply value-based approval workflow
- Approve selected quote/vendor
- Maintain approval traceability

### Step 5: Create Purchase Order (PO)

- Create PO from approved quote
- Confirm item, price, quantity, taxes, and delivery location
- Send PO to vendor

### Step 6: Track Order

- Track acknowledgment and shipment updates
- Monitor expected delivery vs actual timeline
- Follow up on delays

### Step 7: Receive Goods

- Receive parts against PO
- Validate quantity and condition
- Record partial or full receipt
- Raise discrepancy if mismatch exists

### Step 8: Update Inventory and Close

- Update stock records through goods receipt
- Allocate parts to job if required
- Close PO lines after full reconciliation

## 4. Procurement Status Flow (Typical)

Common status pattern:

- Requirement Raised
- Inquiry Sent
- Quotes Received
- Pending Approval
- Approved
- PO Issued
- Partially Received
- Fully Received
- Closed
- Cancelled

Use consistent statuses for reporting and vendor SLA tracking.

## 5. Approval and Control Rules

Core controls:

- Approval threshold by order value
- Dual approval for high-value or exception purchases
- Restricted vendor override access
- Mandatory reason for non-lowest quote selection
- Audit trail for PO changes after issuance

## 6. Receiving and Reconciliation Rules

At receiving stage, verify:

- PO number and vendor match
- Received quantity vs ordered quantity
- Unit quality and specification
- Damaged or short quantity handling
- Receipt references attached

Do not finalize receipt without physical verification.

## 7. Exceptions and Restrictions

Operational restrictions:

- No PO without approved requirement
- No direct receive without PO linkage (except controlled emergency flow)
- No invoice approval without received/validated goods
- No silent edits on approved PO values

These restrictions prevent financial leakage and stock mismatches.

## 8. Procurement KPIs (Recommended)

Track at least:

- Quote turnaround time
- PO cycle time
- On-time delivery rate
- Fill rate (ordered vs received)
- Price variance
- Rejection/return rate

## 9. Validation Checklist

After procurement cycle, confirm:

- Requirement linkage is valid
- Quote comparison and approval are documented
- PO reflects approved terms
- Received quantity is reconciled
- Inventory updated correctly
- PO closure is complete and auditable

## Executive Summary

Procurement Management ensures every required part/service is sourced with control.

Flow:

Requirement -> Inquiry -> Quotes -> Approval -> PO -> Receive -> Inventory Update -> Close

It protects cost control, supply continuity, and operational reliability in automotive workflows.
